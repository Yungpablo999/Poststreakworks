# **A. Document Purpose and How to Use It**

This document is the working blueprint for turning Post Streak from an ambitious idea into a focused product, a validated Nigerian startup and, if the evidence supports it, a continent-wide creator-economy platform. It describes the final platform vision while preventing the founders from attempting to build everything at once.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
|  | **The governing rule**  Build the smallest version that produces repeated creator success. Unlock the next product layer only after the required user behaviour and business evidence appear. |

## **How the founders should use this document**

* Treat the MVP section as the build contract. Features placed in the “later” column should not quietly enter the first release.
* Use every phase gate as a decision point. If retention, safety or completed outcomes are weak, fix the product before increasing marketing spend.
* Convert the roadmap into weekly tasks. Every task must have one owner, one deadline and one definition of done.
* Review assumptions monthly. Costs, platform policies and user preferences must be validated before money is committed.
* Keep Polykoe Voices and Post Streak operationally separate: separate roadmaps, accounts, repositories, records and investor materials.

# **1. The Post Streak Vision**

*The company is bigger than scheduling posts.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
|  | **Vision statement**  Post Streak will become the platform where African creators build their content, professional network, audience, income and creator businesses. |

## **The simple explanation**

Post Streak is an all-in-one creator growth platform that tells creators what useful action to take today, helps them create content, matches them with collaborators, shows what is working and connects them to opportunities to earn.

## **The mission**

Make it easier for talented African creators to become consistent, find serious collaborators, grow professionally and earn sustainable income—without needing several disconnected tools or an established industry network.

## **The long-term company outcome**

* A daily-use creator application with strong habit and community loops.
* A trusted professional identity and reputation system for creators.
* A network matching creators, production talent, agencies and brands.
* A marketplace where creator work, campaigns and services are bought and completed.
* A cross-border business layer for contracts, payments, commerce and professional records.
* A local intelligence system that understands creators, trends and commercial outcomes across African markets.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
|  | **What Post Streak is not**  It is not merely a caption generator, a generic social scheduler, a dating-style swipe app or another public social feed. Each may be a feature, but none is the company. |

# **2. The Customer Problem**

## **Primary creator problems**

| **Problem** | **What the creator experiences** | **Product response** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inconsistency** | They intend to post but run out of ideas, motivation or time. | Personalised missions, realistic plans, streaks and accountability. |
| **Generic advice** | Most tools do not understand the creator’s niche, voice, market or stage. | Creator context, saved voice, local templates and feedback loops. |
| **Difficult collaboration** | Finding trustworthy people with compatible skills and availability is informal and slow. | Professional matching, verification, briefs, chat, tasks and reviews. |
| **Weak growth insight** | Analytics show numbers but rarely explain the next useful action. | Plain-language diagnosis, experiments and weekly recommendations. |
| **Limited opportunities** | Creators struggle to find legitimate campaigns, UGC jobs and industry access. | Verified opportunity marketplace and Creator Passport. |
| **Fragmented business tools** | Planning, portfolios, invoices, contracts and payments sit in different tools. | A creator-business workspace added after product-market fit. |
| **Trust and payment risk** | Collaborations may be unsafe, unpaid or poorly defined. | Verification, records, reporting, agreements and compliant payment flows. |

## **Brand and agency problems**

* Finding relevant emerging creators requires manual searching and unreliable screenshots.
* Follower count is used as a shortcut even when reliability, audience fit and content ability matter more.
* Campaign communication, approvals, deadlines and payments are spread across email, WhatsApp and spreadsheets.
* Brands struggle to compare performance and rehire dependable creators efficiently.

# **4. Initial Target Users**

The initial product should serve Nigerian TikTok and Instagram creators with roughly 1,000–50,000 followers who take content creation seriously but do not yet have a full team, reliable brand pipeline or mature operating system.

| **Persona** | **Needs** | **Why valuable early** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Emerging creator** | Ideas, consistency, feedback, first collaborations and first paid opportunities. | Large group with strong need and natural peer invitations. |
| **Growing micro-creator** | Professional systems, stronger collaborators, media kit and campaigns. | Can demonstrate measurable growth and commercial value. |
| **Creator-service professional** | Clients, portfolio, briefs, scheduling and reliable payment. | Makes the collaboration network useful. |
| **Social media manager** | Manage multiple creators or small-business accounts. | Later professional subscription and team use. |
| **Local brand marketer** | Find relevant creators and manage campaigns. | Higher willingness to pay than most emerging creators. |

## **Who should not control the first roadmap**

* Large celebrity influencers who already have agents and production teams.
* Every small business that merely wants a scheduler.
* People who want a public entertainment feed but do not create professionally.
* Every African country simultaneously.
* Children and young teenagers; an 18+ initial product is operationally safer.

# **5. Product Principles**

| **Principle** | **Practical meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| **Outcome before feature** | A feature matters only when it helps users complete useful creator work. |
| **Daily clarity** | A creator should know the most useful next action within seconds. |
| **Network with purpose** | Matching must produce professional collaboration, not endless swiping. |
| **Trust by design** | Safety, verification, reporting and records are core product features. |
| **Local first, not local only** | Understand Nigeria deeply while designing country-by-country localisation. |
| **Low-data performance** | Remain usable on modest Android devices and inconsistent networks. |
| **AI that learns** | Personalisation and creator control matter more than unlimited generic outputs. |
| **Manual before automated** | Operate processes manually before building complex marketplace automation. |
| **Evidence unlocks expansion** | Retention and outcomes determine when the next layer is built. |

2. They invite collaborators and service professionals.

3. Completed work generates portfolios, reputation and network data.

4. Brands join to access relevant, verified creators.

5. Paid opportunities make Post Streak more valuable to creators.

6. Transactions and results improve recommendations and attract more brands.

7. Network density supports expansion into new cities and countries.

# **7. Competitive Advantage and Moat**

Captions, calendars, dashboards, streak counters and swipe interfaces can be copied. The company must build advantages that become stronger with usage.

| **Moat** | **How it is built** | **Why it becomes defensible** |

| **Collaboration graph** | Completed projects, compatibility, response patterns and reviews. | The system learns which people work successfully together. |

# **10. Home and Daily Growth System**

## **Home screen priorities**

* Today’s mission with a clear action.
* Current streak and weekly progress.
* One recommended collaborator or opportunity—not an overwhelming feed.
* Upcoming content or campaign deadline.
* A short insight from recent activity.
* A visible route to create, save or reschedule the task.

| **Mission category** | **Example** |
| --- | --- |
| **Ideation** | Create five hooks for a recurring fashion series. |
| **Production** | Film two short videos from saved scripts. |
| **Publishing** | Finish and publish the content scheduled for today. |
| **Engagement** | Respond thoughtfully to ten high-value comments. |
| **Collaboration** | Send a complete brief to one compatible creator. |
| **Growth experiment** | Test a direct opening instead of a long introduction. |
| **Business** | Complete your media kit or send an invoice. |
| **Recovery** | Choose one unfinished draft and move it forward. |

## **Mission rules**

* Use niche, stage, goal, available time and prior completions.
* Avoid forcing daily publishing when the realistic schedule is lower.
* Balance creation, analysis, collaboration and recovery.
* Allow replacement, simplification, rescheduling and rest days.
* Never claim a mission guarantees views or followers.

# **12. Streaks, Motivation and Gamification**

A streak should represent meaningful creator progress rather than forcing a post every day. Qualifying actions can include completing a mission, advancing a project, finishing a collaboration task, publishing scheduled work or completing a professional opportunity action.

| **Mechanic** | **Purpose** | **Safeguard** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Daily streak** | Create habit and momentum. | Flexible mission types and rest options. |
| **Weekly target** | Support creators who cannot act daily. | Three-, four- or five-day goals. |
| **Streak freeze** | Protect progress during absence. | Limited availability preserves meaning. |
| **Comeback mode** | Reduce shame after inactivity. | Small restart missions. |
| **XP and levels** | Show long-term progress. | Reward completed work, not spending. |
| **Badges** | Recognise and share milestones. | Avoid false claims about skill or income. |
| **Squad streak** | Create accountability and referrals. | Prevent spam and unhealthy pressure. |
| **Challenges** | Create time-bound community events. | Transparent judging and prize terms. |

# **57. Unit Economics**

| **Metric** | **Formula** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Creator CAC** | Creator growth spend ÷ activated creators. | Downloads without activation mislead. |
| **Brand CAC** | Sales and marketing cost ÷ new paying brands. | Must be supported by gross profit. |
| **Gross margin** | Revenue minus direct AI, payment, hosting and fulfilment costs. | Shows whether revenue supports the company. |
| **Creator LTV** | Monthly gross profit × expected paid lifetime. | Determines sustainable acquisition spend. |
| **Take rate** | Platform fee ÷ gross transaction value. | Measures marketplace monetisation. |
| **Campaign contribution** | Campaign revenue minus direct operating cost. | Prevents loss-making pilots looking successful. |
| **Payback period** | Acquisition cost ÷ monthly contribution. | Shows how quickly growth spend returns. |

## **Economics discipline**

* Track free-user AI cost.
* Track moderation and support minutes.
* Separate revenue from pass-through creator payments.
* Measure GMV and net revenue separately.
* Do not treat investment as revenue.
* Review cohort profitability.

# **73. Interview Guide**

## **Opening**

*“We are researching how creators plan, collaborate and grow. We are not trying to sell you anything during this conversation. I want to understand what you actually do today.”*

## **Behaviour questions**

1. Tell me about the last piece of content you planned and published.

2. How often do you intend to post, and how often do you actually post?

3. What usually stops you from creating consistently?

4. Where did your last five ideas come from?

5. Show me the tools, notes or chats you currently use.

6. Tell me about the last collaboration you attempted.

7. How did you find the person, and what went well or badly?

8. Have you earned money from content? Walk me through the last opportunity.

9. How do brands or clients contact and evaluate you?

10. Which creator tools do you pay for, and why?

11. What would make you open a creator app several times per week?

12. What would make you invite another creator?

13. What would you never trust an app to do?

14. What is the most important creator problem you would pay to solve?

## **Avoid**

* “Do you like our idea?”
* Asking willingness to pay without demonstrating value.
* Explaining every feature before learning the workflow.
* Treating compliments as purchase intent.
* Interviewing only encouraging friends.
